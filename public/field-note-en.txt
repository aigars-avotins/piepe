WHAT IS A BRACKET FUNGUS?
In a forest, a bracket fungus may form a shelf on a trunk or fallen branch. This fruiting body is only part of the fungus: a network of fine threads, called mycelium, can spread through the wood.

A careful look can start with simple questions: is the bracket firm or soft, solitary or clustered, on standing wood or a fallen branch? Such details help describe an encounter, but they are not enough to name a species. Field note 001 offers three ways to practise that attention: looking at the surface, considering what lies beneath, and carrying a different gaze back into daily life.

A fruiting body
The same fungus can form more than one bracket. Some fruiting bodies last a season; others persist and add growth over several years.

A hidden underside
Many bracket fungi have tiny pores underneath. Spores form on the surfaces of tubes opening into these pores and are released into the air. Not every bracket has pores.

Wood becomes a beginning
Many wood-decaying fungi help recycle nutrients by breaking down wood. Some also grow in living trees and can weaken their wood. A bracket alone does not tell the whole story.
